Problem

Why broad pilots lose momentum

  • Unclear source boundaryTeams cannot tell which knowledge is current or approved.
  • Unclear decision ownerNo one owns review, escalation, or the next release decision.
  • No working baselineA convincing demo is not compared with the current process.
  • No production planMonitoring, permissions, feedback, and change controls are added late.
Solution

How we run a bounded pilot

  • One workflow and ownerDefine users, decisions, baseline, and unacceptable outcomes.
  • Grounded source pathConnect curated knowledge, identity, permissions, and citations.
  • Representative user testsReview misses, edge cases, corrections, and review effort.
  • Promotion criteriaAgree on monitoring, incident routes, ownership, and release conditions.

Pilot to production

Build the path before scaling the idea.

The pilot and production design are planned together. Each stage produces evidence for the next decision.

01

Frame

Name one workflow, its users, decision owner, baseline, review boundary, and outcomes that would stop the pilot.

02

Ground

Curate sources, connect identity and permissions, and define retrieval, citations, and human approval gates.

03

Test

Run representative scenarios with real users. Compare results with the baseline and record misses, edge cases, and corrections.

04

Operationalize

Release in stages, monitor quality and usage, assign owners, document incidents, and govern model, prompt, and source changes.

Questions

Clear expectations before a pilot.

The right architecture depends on your data, users, risk tolerance, and operating environment. These answers describe our default approach, not a universal guarantee.

Can the assistant use only our approved data?

We can ground solutions in curated documents, knowledge bases, and approved system data. Source coverage, freshness, retrieval design, and model behavior still affect usefulness. Generated output may be incomplete or incorrect, so material decisions and external communications need review.

How are permissions and sensitive information handled?

We design retrieval and actions around identity, role, source classification, and existing access rules. Exact controls depend on connected systems and approved policy. Access decisions, review events, and source use can be recorded to support governance and accountability.

Where does human review fit into the workflow?

We define review points based on consequence, not novelty. People can inspect supporting evidence, edit drafts, and approve high-impact actions. More repetitive steps can run automatically only when their scope and failure handling are explicitly approved.

How do we monitor and govern the system after launch?

The operating plan defines evaluation sets, monitoring signals, owners, escalation routes, and change controls. After release, teams review quality, user feedback, access events, and incidents before adjusting or expanding use. These controls support governance; they do not guarantee accuracy or regulatory compliance.
